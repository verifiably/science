# Discussion structure

## Standard

1. Critical analysis: current position, strengths, weaknesses, assumptions,
   alternative explanations and confounders together in one analysis.
2. Evidence that would decide between alternatives.
3. Prioritised follow-ups.
4. Synthesis.

## Q&A

One section per specific question: a focused answer and what supports it.
These replace the Standard analysis sections; follow-ups and synthesis remain.

## Double-blind

1. Agent analysis, minted before requesting or reading the person's draft.
2. Person's verbatim draft, minted with attribution.
3. Synthesis citing both pinned notes: agreements, disagreements, and points
   raised by only one side. State that record order proves the agent's analysis
   was committed first, not what the agent had seen before the session.

The first and third notes use the relevant Standard or Q&A sections. Record the
three notes in that order; do not call the result double-blind if the person's
draft was already in the conversation.

## Failure checks

Every discussion gives at least one alternative explanation or confounder, or
says none was found and why. Name evidence that would decide, and label
speculation. Agreement without criticism, “more research is needed” as the
conclusion, and treating one result as settling a claim are failures.
